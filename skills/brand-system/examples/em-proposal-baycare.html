01 — The opportunity

What we heard, and what we recommend.

What you came to us with

BayCare runs eight clinics across the Geelong region. Patient triage is currently handled by reception staff at each clinic — phone-based, manual, with significant variation in how acuity is assessed and which clinician a patient ends up with.

Three problems cost time and trust: appointments mis-booked at first contact (estimated 18% of urgent cases), reception staff burnout from triage decisions outside their training, and patients walking away from the booking process before they're scheduled.

What we heard underneath that

The harder problem isn't the workflow — it's the inconsistency. Network-wide, your clinicians can't trust that the appointment in front of them was correctly triaged. That breaks downstream scheduling, escalation paths, and clinical confidence.

An AI-led triage layer needs to do two things: get the assessment right at first contact, and create an auditable trail every clinician trusts.

"We don't have a booking problem. We have a confidence problem. By the time a patient sees a clinician, no one's sure the right person is in the room."
Dr. Priya Mehta · Network Director, BayCare · Discovery workshop, 24 April 2026

Our recommendation

An AI triage assistant integrated into BayCare's existing booking flow. Patients describe symptoms in their own language; the model assesses acuity using a clinician-validated framework, books the appropriate appointment type with the right clinician, and writes an auditable triage note that arrives in the clinician's view before the appointment. Three-month post-launch tuning included so the model improves on BayCare's actual case mix, not synthetic data.
